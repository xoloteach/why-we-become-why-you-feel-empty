## Short 1: The Arrival Fallacy (Why Finishing Leaves You Empty)
**Target Duration:** ~45 seconds (128 words)  
**Spoken Segment (from Paragraphs 18–22):**

> Achievement answers the question: what have I accomplished? Fulfillment asks: why does this matter?
> 
> Sometimes we don't actually want the things we're chasing. We want the feeling we believe those things will give us. 
> 
> We want money because we imagine that once we have enough, we'll never feel anxious again. We chase status believing that once people respect us, we'll finally feel secure.
> 
> But an achievement cannot heal an insecurity. You cross the finish line, you get the title, but the feeling you were expecting doesn't last. 
> 
> Because the finish line was never the solution. It was just another starting line.

## Short 2: Hedonic Adaptation (Why Good Things Turn Invisible)
**Target Duration:** ~42 seconds (119 words)  
**Spoken Segment (from Paragraphs 4–8):**

> Think about something you own today that you once wished for. 
> 
> When you first got it, you felt genuine gratitude. You thought: this is exactly where I wanted to be.
> 
> But slowly, something shifted. Your apartment became just another place you lived. The phone you couldn't wait to buy became an ordinary tool. 
> 
> This isn't personal failure. Your brain is designed to adapt. Familiar things fade into the background so you can stay alert for new challenges.
> 
> But the better your life becomes, the faster your improvements turn invisible. The baseline resets—and ordinary comfort feels like emptiness.

## Short 3: The Contrast Trap (Comparing Your Reality to Highlights)
**Target Duration:** ~44 seconds (125 words)  
**Spoken Segment (from Paragraphs 9–14):**

> You sit with your morning coffee. Your room is warm, your day is peaceful.
> 
> Then you open your phone.
> 
> In thirty seconds, you see someone having breakfast in Manhattan, someone traveling through Tokyo, and someone launching a company.
> 
> Your physical reality didn't change at all. Your coffee is still warm. But your internal reference point just shifted ten thousand miles away.
> 
> You see their highlight reel, not the difficult conversations or quiet doubts behind it. 
> 
> You're comparing your unedited behind-the-scenes to someone else's polished trailer. No wonder you feel like you're falling behind.
